# How buying a life insurance policy works in India (process knowledge)

Use this for "what happens next?", medical tests, tobacco, disclosure, approval, free-look and claims questions.
If a product document says something more specific, the product document wins.

## 1. From interest to policy
1. **Advisor call.** An advisor confirms the plan, the cover amount (sum assured), the policy term and the exact
   premium. Premium depends on age, gender, cover amount, term, health, lifestyle and tobacco use.
2. **Proposal form.** The customer fills in the insurer's proposal form: personal details, income, occupation,
   health history, lifestyle habits (tobacco, alcohol), existing life policies, and a nominee.
3. **KYC.** Identity and address proof, a photograph, and usually income proof for higher cover amounts.
4. **Medical underwriting (if required).** The insurer decides whether medical tests are needed based on age,
   cover amount, policy term and the answers in the proposal form. Tests may be a tele-medical or video call,
   a home visit for samples, or a visit to a diagnostic centre. Typical tests: height, weight, blood pressure,
   blood and urine tests, and for some people an ECG or other tests. The insurer arranges them and normally
   pays for tests done in India.
5. **Underwriting decision.** The insurer can: accept at standard rates; accept with an extra premium (for example
   for tobacco use or a health condition) or modified terms; postpone; or decline. Under IRDAI's 2024 Master
   Circular, the insurer must ask for any extra information in one go within 7 days of receiving the proposal,
   decide within 7 days of receiving that information, and give reasons if it does not accept the proposal.
6. **Premium and policy issue.** Once accepted and the premium is paid, the policy is issued.

## 2. Tobacco use and honest disclosure
- Insurers price tobacco users differently. Tobacco includes cigarettes, beedis, gutka, pan with tobacco,
  chewing tobacco, and nicotine products; even occasional use usually counts. The look-back period is set by
  each insurer (commonly the last 12 months; some plans use longer, e.g. 5 years).
- Medical tests can include a cotinine test (a nicotine by-product found in urine, blood or saliva), which
  shows recent tobacco or nicotine use.
- **Always disclose fully and honestly**: tobacco, alcohol, health conditions, medicines, family history,
  hazardous hobbies or jobs, and existing policies. Non-disclosure or wrong information can lead to the claim
  being rejected or the policy being cancelled.
- **Section 45, Insurance Act 1938:** within 3 years from the policy date (or risk commencement, revival or
  rider date, whichever is latest) the insurer can question the policy for fraud or for misstatement or
  suppression of a material fact. After 3 years the policy cannot be called in question on any ground.
  If a policy is repudiated for misstatement (not fraud), the insurer must give reasons in writing and return
  the premiums collected within 90 days.

## 3. After the policy is issued
- **Free-look period:** 30 days from receiving the policy document (life policies with a term of 1 year or
  more). The customer can cancel and get the premium back, minus proportionate risk premium for the days
  covered, medical examination costs and stamp duty.
- **Grace period:** 15 days for monthly premiums; 30 days for quarterly, half-yearly and yearly premiums. Cover
  continues during the grace period.
- **Nomination:** mandatory. One or more nominees can be named with percentage shares, and changed at any time.
- **Lapse and revival:** if premiums aren't paid, the policy can lapse. It can be revived within the period
  stated in the policy by paying unpaid premiums with interest or a late fee; a health declaration or medical
  test may be needed.
- **Suicide clause:** if death is by suicide within 12 months of the policy start or revival, the nominee
  receives at least 80% of premiums paid (or the fund value for ULIPs), as the product document specifies;
  after 12 months it is treated like any other death claim.

## 4. Death claims (what the family does)
1. **Intimate the claim** to the insurer as soon as possible (phone, email, website, WhatsApp or branch; see the
   insurer notes).
2. **Documents** usually needed: claim form, death certificate, policy details, nominee's ID and address proof,
   bank details (cancelled cheque). For accidental or unnatural death, also the FIR, post-mortem report and
   police report. The insurer may ask for medical records or an employer certificate depending on the case.
3. **IRDAI timelines (2024 Master Circular):** a death claim must be settled within 15 days of intimation if no
   investigation is needed, and within 45 days if it is investigated. For delays, the insurer must pay interest
   at bank rate plus 2% on its own. A claim cannot be rejected or closed only because of missing documents or
   late intimation.
4. Claims are usually looked at more closely if death happens in the first 3 years (see Section 45 above).

## Things the agent must never say
- That a policy will definitely be approved, or that tests will come back fine.
- That a customer can skip mentioning tobacco, alcohol or a health condition.
- Any premium figure.
